The brief

The operator had signed fourteen independent traders into a hall that had previously been a single supermarket unit. Each trader wanted their own identity. The landlord wanted the hall to read as one place. And the four units already trading could not be closed for the works.

What we did

We surveyed the shell and set a common module — one counter height, one fascia depth, one lighting line — then let the traders choose finishes within it. That single decision is why fourteen different businesses still photograph as one hall.

Nine refrigerated and four heated counters were fabricated to the module, with unit fronts in three agreed timbers. Signage ran as a set: fourteen fascias on a shared tray depth, one suspended directory, and bay numbering that matched the lease plan so deliveries could find the right unit.

Working around a live floor

The hall was split into three phases with hoarding that doubled as pre-opening graphics. Noisy and hot works ran between 21:00 and 06:00. Each phase was handed over trading before the next was opened up, so the hall never lost more than four units at a time.

Outcome

Eleven weeks from first fix to the final fascia, with every phase handed over on its programmed date. Three traders have since taken a second unit, and the counters are on a quarterly refrigeration service with us.
